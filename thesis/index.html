Sector thesis · Road passenger transport · India · FY26 to FY31

How India Moves
(Roads edition)

Two of every three rupees Indians spend on fares go to something that runs on a road. Most of that money moves through autos, buses and cabs that no company organises, and the companies that do run vehicles mostly lose money. This report maps the full passenger sector once, then goes deep on road, and names the startups working next to each problem.

November 2026 · Figures are FY26 unless stated · ₹ Cr = crore rupees · Startup funding as stated on Tracxn, November 2026

Executive summaryThe short version

Key findings

  1. Road is the sector. Road fares come to about ₹4.18 lakh crore of ₹6.2 lakh crore, and road vehicles carry 55% of daily trips. Households spend a further ₹17.9 lakh crore a year owning their own two-wheelers and cars.
  2. The largest road pool belongs to nobody. Autos and e-rickshaws take ₹1.68 lakh crore, and 98% of their trips never touch an app. Drivers keep ₹75,600 Cr a year.
  3. Running vehicles loses money and organising them earns it. State buses lose about ₹22,500 Cr in cash a year. Office commute firms and outstation cab networks are the steady earners.
  4. Eight gaps already have funded startups, led by e-auto credit, battery swapping and branded intercity buses. Battery Smart and SUN Mobility alone have raised about $370M between them.
  5. Six bets have no funded player, led by organising autos at the stand and school transport outside big cities, where Tracxn shows only 11 funded Indian startups.

Definitions. Passenger travel here means a person moving by choice. A gap is a problem most sources report and that a startup or company is already moving into. A bet is a reported problem where no company is visibly working at scale. Each problem sits in one list only.

Section 1The whole sector, once

India's passenger transport splits cleanly into six parts by the surface a person travels on, plus one horizontal layer that serves all of them. Each part has its own rule-makers, its own infrastructure owner and its own operators. Open any branch below.

1. Road transport · Centre and states share control · focus of this report
  • Policy and regulation: MoRTH sets the Motor Vehicles Act and vehicle standards; state transport departments and RTOs issue permits, collect road tax and enforce.
  • Infrastructure: NHAI and MoRTH run national highways and expressways; state PWDs and city bodies run the rest.
  • Operations: state road transport undertakings, formal private buses, intermediate public transport (autos, e-rickshaws, taxis, ride-hailing), school and office fleets, informal para-transit, and personal two-wheelers and cars.
2. Rail and urban rapid transit · Centre and joint ventures
  • Ministry of Railways sets intercity policy, MoHUA sets urban transit policy, and the Commission of Railway Safety oversees safety.
  • Indian Railways runs 17 zones and about 70 divisions, with tickets through IRCTC. Bharat Gaurav and luxury tourist trains sit alongside.
  • Metros and light rail run through 50:50 Centre-state companies and PPP concessions; RRTS through NCRTC.
3. Civil aviation · Centralised, deregulated
  • MoCA sets policy. DGCA licenses and enforces safety, AERA sets airport tariffs, BCAS and CISF run security, and AAIB investigates accidents.
  • AAI controls air traffic and public airports; private consortia run the large PPP airports.
  • Full-service and low-cost airlines fly scheduled routes; UDAN commuter airlines, charters and helicopters fly the rest.
4. Waterways and ferries · Centre and coastal states
  • The shipping ministry and DG Shipping regulate safety, IWAI runs inland waterways, and state maritime boards run coastal and local services.
  • Island vessels run through the Shipping Corporation and UT administrations; water metros, ro-pax and river ferries run inland and on the coast.
5. Cable and non-motorised · Local and niche
  • Ropeways and cable cars under MoRTH's Parvatmala programme and state authorities, run by tourism companies and PPP operators.
  • Footpaths, skywalks and cycle tracks built by cities; bicycles, cycle-rickshaws and shared e-bikes and scooters run on them.
6. Enablers across every mode · Horizontal layers
  • Planning: PM Gati Shakti nationally, and Unified Metropolitan Transport Authorities in cities.
  • Digital and payment rails: NCMC cards, UPI for transit, FASTag, ONDC Mobility, Vahan and Sarathi registries, traffic control and e-challans.
  • Supply chain: vehicle and EV makers, rail coach factories, shipyards, maintenance shops, fuel and charging networks, scrappage centres.
  • Finance and response: transit budgets, multilateral lenders, vehicle NBFCs, IRDAI-regulated insurance, 108 ambulances and rescue forces.

Exotic and niche travel

These modes are real but small, and none changes the picture. Ocean cruises carried 4.71 lakh passengers in FY24, worth roughly ₹830 Cr in FY25, and Cordelia Cruises holds about four-fifths of that. The Cruise Bharat Mission targets 10 lakh cruise passengers by 2029. River cruises ran 443 voyages on national waterways in FY25, up 19%.

Space travel is not yet a market. Gaganyaan's first crewed flight is targeted for 2027, and no Indian company sells space tourism. Elsewhere, Kedarnath helicopters averaged about 215 flights a day in the 2025 season, Bharat Gaurav tourist trains carried over 96,000 people in 2023, only two UDAN seaplane water aerodromes were working as of October 2025, and a pipeline of 250+ ropeways is planned.

Moving live animals, an estimated ₹26,500 Cr a year, is freight. An animal does not choose its trip, so it is not passenger travel and is counted nowhere in this report.

Section 2Why road

Indians make about 165.5 crore trips a day longer than a kilometre. Road vehicles carry 55% of them: two-wheelers 31%, buses 11%, autos 9% and cars 4%. Walking and cycling carry another 42%, and metro, rail and app cabs about 1% each.

The money follows. Of ₹6,20,066 Cr in fares, about ₹4,17,716 Cr, or 67%, goes to road services. Air takes ₹1.12 lakh crore and all rail, metro included, ₹90,400 Cr. And road fares are the smaller half of road spending: owning private vehicles costs households ₹17.9 lakh crore a year, 2.9 times all fares combined.

Section 3Rules and roads

3.1 Who makes the rules

Road transport is a shared subject. The Centre writes the Motor Vehicles Act, vehicle safety and emission standards and, since 2025, the Motor Vehicles Aggregator Guidelines. States decide almost everything that touches a business: who gets a permit, how much road tax a vehicle pays, which fares apply and how hard the rules are enforced. So one national law turns into many state markets.

Bike taxis show how far apart they sit. Maharashtra allows electric bike taxis only, with a 15 km trip cap and a full switch off petrol by July 2027. Karnataka's are tied up in court, Delhi has not notified final rules, and Tamil Nadu has none. The aggregator guidelines give drivers at least 80% of the fare and cap surge at twice the base fare, but most states have not enforced them.

Labour rules are moving fastest. Karnataka and Telangana passed gig worker acts, and five states charge platforms 1 to 5% for gig welfare. The levy falls on payouts, so subscription apps where riders pay drivers directly mostly escape it for now.

Money from the Centre shapes what gets bought. PM E-DRIVE subsidises electric commercial vehicles, and the PM e-Bus Sewa payment fund guarantees operators get paid on 38,000+ electric buses. Diesel and CNG bus contracts sit outside it.

3.2 The roads themselves

NHAI and MoRTH own national highways and expressways, and states and cities own everything else. Highways are where private money already earns: toll collections reached about ₹82,900 Cr, up 14%, over 4.45 billion FASTag transactions. Faster highways are also what made overnight sleeper buses and outstation cabs worth running.

The cost shows up in safety. Road crashes killed 1.83 lakh people in 2025. Two-wheeler riders were 46% of the dead and pedestrians 21%, while buses carry 11% of trips and account for 2% of deaths.

Section 4Road operations, segment by segment

The six operating segments below cover every paid and unpaid road trip without overlap. Five are sized in fares; personal vehicles are sized by what owners spend.

4.1 State road transport undertakings · ₹66,000 Cr

India's 58 state bus corporations, including BMTC, BEST, DTC, MSRTC and KSRTC, run about 1.5 lakh buses and carry about 7 crore riders a day. Intercity routes bring in ₹47,500 Cr and city routes ₹18,500 Cr. Together they lose about ₹22,500 Cr in cash a year. Free bus travel for women in Karnataka, Tamil Nadu, Telangana and Delhi has raised ridership sharply and made states, not riders, the main payer.

4.2 Formal private buses · ₹47,916 Cr

Private operators own about 87% of India's buses. On intercity routes, 6,858 operators carried 28.72 crore passengers in FY26 for ₹27,416 Cr, up 24.5%. AC sleepers are 55 to 60% of trips and earn 12 to 15% margins, yet 81% of tickets are still sold offline. In cities, private stage carriages and charters take about ₹20,500 Cr, mainly in Kerala, West Bengal and Odisha, where private buses run regular routes.

4.3 Intermediate public transport · ₹2,53,800 Cr

This is the heart of road mobility. Autos and e-rickshaws collect ₹1.68 lakh crore from 70 lakh vehicles, 25 lakh of them electric. A typical auto earns ₹19,900 a month and the driver keeps 45%. Ride-hailing apps handle ₹38,500 Cr across 303 crore rides; bikes and autos are 78% of those rides, and Rapido runs about half. Outstation cabs take ₹37,500 Cr, with 70% booked through local agents, and self-drive rentals and driver leases ₹9,800 Cr.

4.4 School and office fleets · ₹50,000 Cr

School and college buses take ₹27,500 Cr, carrying 1.5 crore students at ₹1,500 to ₹2,200 a month and growing about 8% a year. New rules require GPS and CCTV. Office commute takes ₹22,500 Cr, paid by employers on multi-year contracts, and is one of the few profitable road segments; MoveInSync runs at a 34% margin.

4.5 Informal para-transit · not sized

Below the permit system sit modified rural vehicles such as the jugaad and chhakda, unlicensed vans and about 4.75 lakh unregistered e-rickshaws in UP, Bihar, Assam, Delhi and West Bengal. School vans alone are worth about ₹5,000 Cr. Nobody has counted this segment properly, which is itself the finding: the transport most of rural India uses is invisible to every dataset in this report.

4.6 Personal two-wheelers and cars · ₹17.9 lakh crore of ownership cost

The scooter is the price every paid service has to beat. Two-wheelers carry 31% of trips and cars 4%. Owning and running them costs households ₹17.9 lakh crore a year, of which about ₹1.25 lakh crore overlaps with commercial use. Used vehicles add ₹4 lakh crore of resale, used cars sell 1.4 times as often as new ones, and the vehicle loan book stands at ₹20.5 lakh crore.

Section 5Who makes money on the road

Road operators that run their own vehicles lose money, and the losses sit mostly with the state. The private profit sits with firms that organise other people's vehicles under contract, and with drivers.

The largest pile of money belongs to no company: auto drivers take home ₹75,600 Cr a year. Electric is cheaper per km in every class. Over five years an e-auto costs ₹3.29 a km against ₹5.51 for CNG, leaving the driver ₹8,965 a month better off; an EV cab costs ₹6.00 a km against ₹7.58. Lenders that track the vehicle keep bad loans at 1.5 to 2.5%, against 3.5 to 5.5% for others.

Capital has followed the platforms. Outside investors put $1.15 billion into mobility from FY23 to November 2026 across 86 deals: ride-hailing took 52%, EV fleets and shared bikes 33%, and bus and commute tech 15%. Rapido is valued at $3 billion, while Ola has been marked down from $7.3 billion to about $1.5 billion.

Section 7Gaps: problems startups are already moving into

Each gap is a road problem reported across sources where funded companies are already building. The startups listed are the main ones working on or next to the problem, with total funding as Tracxn states it. Tracxn's own pages sometimes disagree; where they do, the higher figure is shown.

Gap 1

Expensive credit for e-autos

Problem
Three in four new e-autos are bought on credit at 14 to 30%. The loan or rent takes 24% of what an auto earns.
What is still open
Credit for drivers without a history, and pricing the battery's resale value.
Wrong if
Battery values collapse after a few summers.

Section 8Bets: problems nobody is solving

Each bet is a reported road problem where Tracxn shows no funded startup working on it directly. The startups listed sit next to the problem and are the likeliest to move in.

Bet 1

Organise the offline auto economy where it lives

Problem
₹1.68 lakh crore and 70 lakh vehicles run almost entirely offline; 98% of auto trips never touch an app.
Why nobody has
Apps try to pull rides online. Nobody serves the stand, the shared route and the cash or UPI fare as they already work.
Who would pay
Drivers, who already pay ₹20 to ₹450 a day for passes and leases.
Wrong if
Drivers won't pay for anything that doesn't bring them rides.

YuluEV two-wheeler fleet for gig workers$198M

Bet 2

A safe first and last mile for women

Problem
77% of women say poor access to and from transit keeps them off it. When Karnataka made buses free, women's daily trips nearly doubled.
Why nobody has
Women-only offers such as Rapido's Bike Pink cost more and drew complaints, and women are 0.8% of drivers in one sample.
Who would pay
Employers or the state, more likely than women themselves.
Wrong if
It can't match the convenience of what exists.
Adjacent startups on Tracxn

No funded startup found. The nearest activity is a feature inside Rapido, not a company.

Bet 3

School transport beyond the big cities

Problem
₹27,500 Cr of school buses plus about ₹5,000 Cr of vans carry 1.5 crore students, and new rules require GPS and CCTV.
Why nobody has
Schools treat it as a chore, and the market is fragmented. Tracxn lists 72 Indian school transport startups, of which only 11 are funded; SafeTrack and Zuwagon have shut.
Who would pay
Parents, who already pay ₹1,500 to ₹2,200 a month.
Wrong if
Schools keep it informal and rules go unenforced.

Bet 4

Buses for the 400+ cities with none

Problem
More than 400 cities have no organised public transport, and every missing bus trip becomes an auto ride or a scooter.
Why nobody has
Funded bus companies chase large-city contracts inside the payment fund. Small cities can't pay reliably.
Who would pay
States, per km, where payment is guaranteed.
Wrong if

Bet 5

One bundle of services for the driver

Problem
Drivers buy finance, insurance, charging and bookings from different places. Only 19% of women auto drivers in one survey have insurance, and 93% carry loans.
Why nobody has
Each company serves one piece: lenders lend, apps book, swappers swap.
Who would pay
Drivers, with platforms pushed by gig welfare laws to cover them.
Wrong if
The pieces stay cheaper bought separately.
Adjacent startups on Tracxn

No driver-specific insurance or welfare startup found. RevFin, Battery Smart and Rapido each serve one piece and could widen.

Bet 6

Rural and informal para-transit

Problem
Jugaads, chhakdas, unlicensed vans and about 4.75 lakh unregistered e-rickshaws carry much of rural and small-town India, uncounted and uninsured.
Why nobody has
The vehicles sit outside permits, so lenders, insurers and apps can't see them.
Who would pay
Unclear. Possibly states paying to formalise, or makers of cheap electric replacements.
Wrong if
Formalising raises costs enough to push riders back onto two-wheelers.
Adjacent startups on Tracxn

None found.

Section 9Risks

  • Battery value. Gaps 1 and 2 and Bet 1 rest on what an EV battery is worth after years of heat.
  • State payment. Any business paid by states depends on the payment fund or suffers 2 to 6 month delays.
  • Regulation. Bike taxis are legal state by state, and gig welfare levies could extend to daily passes.
  • Price sensitivity. The fastest growth is in the cheapest rides, where margins are thinnest.
  • Data. Startup funding comes from Tracxn pages, which sometimes disagree with each other; the informal segment has no reliable count.
